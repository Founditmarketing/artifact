# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Northwest Arkansas residents and small-business owners storing personal
effects, business inventory, boats, RVs, and vehicles. Most arrive after a
Google search or a referral. They are price-sensitive but tired of being
burned by chain operators' teaser rates that escalate after ninety days. They
want a real person to answer the phone, a fair monthly bill that does not
move, and a facility they can drive to without thinking about it.

## Product Purpose

Artifacts Self Storage is a family-owned, three-facility self-storage operator
in Rogers, Arkansas. The site's job is to win the local search, convince a
sceptical visitor that "honest pricing, no spikes" is a real commitment and
not marketing copy, and route them into a Storedge reservation. Success is a
measurable lift in online reserves, a measurable drop in "is this a teaser?"
support calls, and a brand that local managers are proud to point to.

## Brand Personality

Archival. Earnest. Quietly proud.

The site reads like a hand-set field catalogue or a small museum's gallery
guide. The voice is plainspoken; the typography does the confidence. Authority
comes from precision (accession codes, dimensioned ledgers, section markers)
and from refusing the self-storage industry's bad habits.

## Reference DNA

Reference atlases and museum archives. Specifically: gallery wall texts at
Cooper Hewitt and the V&A; the plate-and-caption rhythm of mid-century
exhibition catalogues; the typographic precision of Cabinet magazine; the
material honesty of Vitsoe and Herman Miller archival photography; the
restrained data display of an old USGS topographic legend.

## Anti-references

- **Chain operators** (Public Storage, Extra Space, CubeSmart): cluttered
  promo strips, stock photography of smiling families next to a roll-up door,
  "Reserve in 60 seconds" pressure typography, garish CTA reds.
- **SaaS-cream marketing**: rounded cards on rounded cards, pastel
  gradients, glassmorphism, "Built for modern teams" tagline shapes.
- **Builder-template realtor sites**: full-bleed video hero, parallax
  scrolling, italicised "Welcome Home" serif, image carousels.
- **Neon, brutalist, Y2K-revival**: dated within twelve months. The catalogue
  motif must read intentional, not trendy.
- **Trendy editorial overload**: massive serif headlines for their own sake,
  marquees on every section, motion for motion's sake.

## Design Principles

1. **Catalogue, never campaign.** Every section reads like an entry, not a
   pitch. Section markers, accession codes, and ledger rules carry the rhythm.
   Specifications outrank adjectives.
2. **Earn confidence through specifics.** Phone numbers, addresses, dimensions,
   hours, the four commitments. Anything vague is cut. The reader should be
   able to plan their visit without leaving the page.
3. **Honesty is a design surface.** "No surprise spikes" appears as structural
   copy, not as a banner. The price ledger shows the real "from" numbers per
   facility, in serif, large, unhedged.
4. **One detail per surface.** Reveal one architectural flourish per section
   (the spine ruler in the hero, the spinning stamp on the photo, the
   registration marks on a plate). Do not repeat them. Density beats fireworks.
5. **Local before scaleable.** This site exists for Rogers, AR. References to
   Beaver Lake, I-49, Mercy Hospital, Pinnacle Hills outrank generic copy. Do
   not sand off the specifics.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. All body text meets 4.5:1 contrast on its background;
large headlines meet 3:1. Motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. Focus
states are visible against every surface (paper, ink, gold, blue). Tap targets
on touch devices meet 44×44 px. Ornamental registration marks, datum ticks,
and crop-marks are `aria-hidden`; photographs carry descriptive alt text. The
catalogue rhythm (section markers, accession codes) is decorative, not
load-bearing for screen-reader navigation, which relies on standard headings
and landmarks.
